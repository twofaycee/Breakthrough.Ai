# AFTER MIDNIGHT — Episode 1 Production Plan

**Visual direction:** Cinematic
**Target runtime:** 3–5 minutes
**Purpose:** First BREAKTHROUGH original proof-of-concept

## Creative standard

Every shot should feel like a frame from a premium mystery/thriller rather than generic AI footage. Favor grounded performances, believable radio-station details, controlled camera movement, practical lighting, silence, and environmental sound.

## Master visual language

- Contemporary, grounded thriller realism
- Deep blacks with cool blue/green nighttime ambience
- Restrained red from the ON AIR sign and equipment indicators
- Soft practical lighting; avoid overly glossy sci-fi lighting
- Natural skin texture and believable facial proportions
- 24fps cinematic motion language
- Shallow depth of field only when it supports the story
- Slow pushes, locked-off dialogue shots, and restrained handheld tension
- Rain on glass, reflections, worn equipment, coffee, paperwork, cables, and small station imperfections
- Supernatural elements remain ambiguous and understated

## Episode shot plan

### Scene 1 — The Last Show of the Night
1. Exterior establishing shot: quiet city street in rain, radio station glowing faintly.
2. Wide interior: Malik alone at the overnight console.
3. Close-up: clock approaching 2:13 A.M.
4. Medium close-up: Malik speaking into microphone.
5. Insert: phone board dark, then one line suddenly illuminates.
6. Close-up: Malik notices the unexpected call.
7. Tight shot: receiver lifted; woman's voice is heard but she is never shown.
8. Reaction close-up: Malik after hearing his name.
9. Insert: call disconnects.

### Scene 2 — The Second Call
10. Phone rings immediately; Malik answers without going on air.
11. Over-shoulder: Malik searches for Bellweather.
12. Screen reflection on Malik's face: no useful result.
13. Close-up: Malik listening as the woman warns him.
14. Practical lights flicker.
15. Tight shot: Malik looks upward.
16. Seven-second tension sequence: lights die.
17. Emergency lighting reveals Malik in darkness.
18. Clock changes to 2:14 A.M.
19. Malik realizes the caller predicted the outage.
20. Hold on empty receiver after the line dies.

### Scene 3 — The Recording
21. Over-shoulder: overnight automation folder.
22. Insert: NIGHTLINE_0213.wav appears.
23. Close-up: created timestamp reads TOMORROW, 2:13 A.M.
24. Malik presses play.
25. Reaction shot as his own voice repeats the earlier broadcast.
26. Slow push toward Malik as the recording says not to answer the next call.
27. Extreme close-up: phone begins ringing.
28. Malik slowly turns toward it.
29. Cut to black.
30. Hold phone ring over black before ending.

## Audio palette

- Continuous rain bed in Scene 1, reduced during dialogue.
- Low electrical/station room tone.
- Isolated telephone ring with strong transient attack.
- Subtle HVAC/computer hum.
- Electrical flicker and power-drop sound in Scene 2.
- No cheap musical stingers.
- Minimal suspense score only if it strengthens the scene.
- Final phone ring continues over black.

## Production order

1. Lock Malik character reference.
2. Build radio-station environment reference.
3. Create keyframes for all 30 shots.
4. Generate motion shots from the approved keyframes.
5. Produce Malik dialogue and woman's dialogue.
6. Build ambience, Foley, and restrained score.
7. Assemble and edit.
8. Add subtitles and title/end card.
9. Export 16:9 master plus vertical promotional cuts.
10. Quality-control every shot before publishing.
