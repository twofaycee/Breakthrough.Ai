# AFTER MIDNIGHT
## Episode 1 — The Town That Isn't There

**Format:** Original BREAKTHROUGH series
**Genre:** Mystery / Thriller
**Target runtime:** 3–5 minutes for the first proof-of-concept cut
**Status:** Production draft

### Series premise
Night-shift radio host **Malik Reed** starts receiving calls from a town that does not appear on any map. Every caller knows details about his life that they should not know. The deeper Malik investigates, the more the broadcasts begin predicting events before they happen.

### Main character
**Malik Reed, 26** — sharp, observant, funny when nervous, and used to being the calm voice on the other side of the microphone. He works the overnight shift at a small independent radio station in a city that never quite sleeps.

### Episode 1 logline
At 2:13 a.m., Malik answers a call from a woman claiming to be broadcasting from Bellweather, a town removed from every public map decades ago. She gives him a warning about an event that happens seconds later.

## SCRIPT

### SCENE 1 — THE LAST SHOW OF THE NIGHT
**INT. RADIO STUDIO — 2:08 A.M.**

Rain taps against the studio windows. The city outside is almost empty.

Malik leans toward the microphone.

**MALIK**
You’re listening to Nightline 88.7. Five minutes left before I hand the morning crew a city that hopefully survived another night.

He checks the clock. 2:08.

The phone board is dark.

Then one line lights up.

2:13 A.M.

Malik frowns.

**MALIK**
That’s weird.

He answers.

**MALIK**
Nightline. You’re on the air.

A woman breathes quietly on the other end.

**WOMAN**
Is Malik Reed there?

Malik straightens.

**MALIK**
Speaking.

**WOMAN**
Don’t say your name again.

Silence.

**MALIK**
Okay. Why?

**WOMAN**
Because they can hear you.

The call drops.

Malik stares at the receiver.

---

### SCENE 2 — THE SECOND CALL
The phone rings again immediately.

Malik answers without going on the air.

**MALIK**
Who is this?

**WOMAN**
Bellweather.

**MALIK**
What?

**WOMAN**
That’s where I’m calling from.

Malik looks toward the studio computer and types the name into a search engine.

No result.

**MALIK**
There’s no Bellweather in this state.

**WOMAN**
Not anymore.

Malik pauses.

**MALIK**
What does that mean?

**WOMAN**
It means you need to leave the station before the lights go out.

The overhead lights flicker.

Malik looks up.

**MALIK**
How long?

**WOMAN**
Seven seconds.

The lights go out.

Darkness.

A beat.

Emergency lights click on.

Malik looks at the clock.

2:14 A.M.

**MALIK**
You knew.

**WOMAN**
I told you.

**MALIK**
How?

**WOMAN**
Because I heard this broadcast last night.

Malik goes still.

**MALIK**
There wasn't a broadcast last night.

**WOMAN**
I know.

The line goes dead.

---

### SCENE 3 — THE RECORDING
Malik checks the station automation computer.

A new audio file has appeared in the overnight folder.

Filename: **NIGHTLINE_0213.wav**

Created: **TOMORROW, 2:13 A.M.**

Malik's face changes.

He presses play.

His own voice comes through the speakers.

**MALIK'S RECORDED VOICE**
You’re listening to Nightline 88.7. Five minutes left before I hand the morning crew a city that hopefully survived another night.

It is the exact sentence he said minutes ago.

Then the recording continues.

**MALIK'S RECORDED VOICE**
If you're hearing this, don't answer the next call.

The studio phone rings.

Malik slowly turns toward it.

CUT TO BLACK.

### END OF EPISODE 1

## VISUAL DIRECTION
- Grounded contemporary thriller rather than fantasy spectacle.
- Nighttime palette: deep blacks, cool blue-green practical light, restrained red from the ON AIR sign.
- Camera language: slow pushes, locked-off frames during dialogue, subtle handheld tension when Malik realizes something is wrong.
- Keep the radio station believable: inexpensive equipment, coffee cup, paperwork, rain-streaked glass, worn desk.
- The supernatural element should remain ambiguous in Episode 1.

## AUDIO DIRECTION
- Rain ambience throughout the opening.
- Low electrical hum from the studio equipment.
- Telephone ring should feel unusually sharp and isolated.
- No musical jump scares; build tension with silence and environmental sound.
- End on the phone ringing over black.
